<h3> Walls in a curved six-dimensional space </h3>
<i>Based on: M. Liefsoens, Hypersurfaces of any homogeneous ℂP³, Annali di Matematica Pura ed Applicata 205, 295-311 (2026).</i>

A sphere looks the same from every point: it is <i>homogeneous</i>. The space ℂP³ is a six-dimensional cousin of the sphere, and it can be curved in a whole family of ways that all keep this "same from every point" property, much like a ball that you squeeze or stretch evenly along a hidden direction.

A <i>hypersurface</i> is a wall of one dimension less inside such a space, like the equator (a circle), inside a sphere.
The paper finds all walls that are as symmetric as possible: those that also look the same everywhere. It does so for the whole family of shapes of ℂP³ at once, instead of one shape at a time.
For one special shape, the <i>nearly Kähler</i> one --- which appears in M-theory --- it also classifies the walls that interact best with the extra structure of this special shape.
